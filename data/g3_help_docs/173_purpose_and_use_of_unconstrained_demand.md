# Purpose and Use of Unconstrained Demand

The RMScollects your property's demand history to calculate unconstrained demand then searches for patterns to create demand models by Forecast Group. It uses the demand models to forecast future unconstrained demand for the entire forecast window by Forecast Group, Room Class, and length of stay.
View the videos in Resources on the right to learn more about unconstrained demand.

What if your actuals don't match whatthe RMSexpected?The RMSis a self-learning system and adjusts constantly.
- During optimization: The system checks its forecasts for the future against what really happened, then it adjusts. For example, if rooms for a future date picked up much slower than expected, the system might adjust down its forecast and change decisions.The RMSchecks  the entire forecasting window, for each Forecast Group, Room Class, and length of stay.
- Updates of the demand models: Approximately every two weeks the system checks for changes in the demand models, like the day of the week demand pattern for a Forecast Group, and monitors future bookings. If the new pattern continues over a long period, like 10 to 12 weeks, the system decides to use the new pattern  in its forecasts.Aside from its regular checks,the RMSchecks the demand models also after some of the changes that trigger theSyncflag. Because it checks all demand models, the resulting forecast changes can seem disproportionate, seeSpecial Eventsfor an example.

- After large demand changes:The RMSconstantly reviews the history to decide if recent or older data produces better forecasts. If the system notices that the demand patterns change quickly, it tends to rely more on recent history to forecast. If patterns change slowly, it relies less on recent history.For example, an economic downturn, the loss of a large corporate account, or a new competitor across the street causes the demand to slow down, compared to historical results. If the slowdown continues for about two weeks,the RMSmight decide to use more recent data to forecast demand. This enables the system to quickly react to changes in overall strength of demand, for example, by forecasting a slow April using the slow preceding months of February and March instead of the strong April of prior years.
