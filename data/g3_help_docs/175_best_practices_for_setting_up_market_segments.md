Why do I set up Market Segments?

# Best Practices for Setting Up Market Segments

You can also view a video about this in the Resources on the right.
To achieve the best possible forecasts and decisions,the RMSforecasts different types of business differently. For example, a fixed-priced market segment has a very different ADR forecast than one that is derived off another rate code. But the system doesn't use your market segments for forecasting because they  often have low volumes of booking data, which leads to higher uncertainty in forecasts.
Instead,the RMScombines market segments with similar characteristics and patterns into Forecast Groups. To find such similar segments,the RMSanalyzes a market segment's pattern and detects some its characteristics. And you either review or assign attributes to a market segment. For example, whether it's fixed-priced or not.
Together, you and the system convert the original market segments imported from yourreservation systeminto Forecast Groups with enough booking data and thus low uncertainty.The RMSthen uses these Forecast Groups, along with Room Classes, to create the best possible forecasts and decisions.
Clean market segments contain business with the same characteristics and patterns. In other words, one set of attributes assigned at themarket segment leveldescribes the majority of business in them. A dirty market segment has rate codes with different characteristics.
Clean market segments helpthe RMSproduce better forecasts due to their lower uncertainty. They also lead to better decisions becausethe RMScan treat business with different behaviors differently in its optimization. For an example, view the  video in the Resources on the right.
Therefore you split market segments that are dirtyandlarge into clean ones by assigning attributes at the rate code level.
Typically, no market segment is 100% clean, for these reasons:
- Mislabeling. For example, a guest books a BAR (or your name for theprimary priced productMain pricing decision that the RMS optimizes. Usually this is the lowest non-restricted product with flexible cancellation policy that anyone can book. The default name is Best Available Rate (BAR), but you can change it in Pricing Configuration.) reservation, but at check-in requests a corporate rate. The agent changes the rate, but does not update the market segment. If the mistake is not corrected,the RMSshows one corporate rate booking in the BAR market segment.
- Misfits. Even without mistakes, market segments may contain one or two small rate codes with different behavior or patterns. These rate codes may not have a perfect market segment match and are placed in the closest fit.
If mislabeled or misfit rate codes make up only a small portion of a market segment or if a market segment is very small,the RMScan handle it. It detects the appropriate patterns and filters out those few rate codes with different patterns.
A market segment is clean enough and you should attributes at the market segment, not the rate code, level if:
- The rate code contains less than 1% of the total property business, or
- Its market segment contains less than 5% of the total property.
Note:The RMSwarns you when you assign attributes at a very low level. Ifthe RMSassigns your attributes, it follows similar rules.
Assigning attributes at the rate code level to fix every mistake has downsides:
- It makes the setup work more complex and longer.
- It leads to more market segments and likely more Forecast Groups. You work at the Forecast Group level, thus your work takes longer.
- More market segments and Forecast Groups means less data in each, making forecasts more difficult.
The RMScreates a Default Market Segment for each split market segment, in other words, whenever attributes are assigned at the rate code level. For example, the CORP market segment is split into CORP_QY and CORP_QS . The system calls the default one CORP_DEF.
For that default market segment, the system assigns the same attributes as the largest split one has. For example, if CORP_QY is has more revenue than _QS, then _DEF has the QY attributes. When you split a new market segment you assign the attributes to _DEF. If you leave any rate codes of the split market segment unassigned,the RMSadds them to the _DEF market segment. You can also click the assign iconafter the market segment to assign rate codes manually to _DEF.
The RMSplaces a new rate code that belongs to a split market segment in its Default Market Segment. That is becausethe RMS:
- Can't know the new rate code's attributes and thus into which of the split segments it belongs.

Cleanmarket segments (if you assigned attributes at the Market Segment Level) don't require a Default Market Segment. For these,the RMSassigns the same attributes to all new rate codes. And if anAttribute Assignment Rulecovers the new rate code,the RMSuses that rule to assign attributes.
Let's say that you have a new rate code that belongs to a market segment, CORPX. CORPX is split at the Rate Code Level into two market segments. One contains rate codes that are linked to BAR (CORPX_QYL). The other contains rate codes that are fixed and not linked to BAR (CORPX_QY).
The RMSdoesn't know whether the new rate code is linked to BAR and belongs to CORPX_QYL or not linked to BAR and belongs to CORPX_QY. Therefore,The RMSassigns all bookings for the new rate code to the Default Market Segment, CORPX_DEF.
The RMSalso creates anUnassigned Rate Code Alertfor you. Follow the Alert links and assign attributes to the new rate code. When you're done, any new bookings for it go into the correct market segment. All previous bookings remain in the Default Market Segment. To decide how to assign attributes to Default Market Segments, ask yourself what rate codes you will create for it in the future. In our example, are future rate codes more likely to belong to CORPX_QYL or CORPX_QY?
Note that you need tocreate and commit Forecast Groupsfor the Default Market Segment. To do this, you receive anUnassigned Market Segment Alert. Follow the links in the Alert. The Alerts arrive with the first booking for the Default Market Segment. That means that you might receive them right away, or they might come later. Either way,  act on the Alerts as soon as possible.
The RMSanalyzes historic and future bookings to detect behaviors and assigns the attributes that align with that behavior. For example, for one market segment, CORP, the system finds that its ADR  follows slightly below the ADR of theprimary priced productMain pricing decision that the RMS optimizes. Usually this is the lowest non-restricted product with flexible cancellation policy that anyone can book. The default name is Best Available Rate (BAR), but you can change it in Pricing Configuration.. In that case it assigns the attributes for Qualified business Linked to BAR. For another market segment, the system finds that the ADR is fixed and it selects Qualified Non-Linked.
It the market segment behavior is consistent, in other words, if the market segment isclean,the RMSassigns attributes at the market segment level. If the patterns differ within a market segment, in other words, if it'sdirty, it splits the market segment into clean ones and assigns attributes at the rate code level. For example, if some of the rate codes of your original CORP market segment are fixed priced and some are linked to BAR, then the system splits CORP into two, CORP_QY and CORP_QYL.
The RMScan't find some behaviors in the data, for example, if qualified business is yieldable or not. That's why, during your initial setup, you review and, if needed, change the attributes that the system assigned, see thereview steps 2. to 6.

### Small Attribute Changes Can Cause Large Forecast Changes

When you change the attributes of a market segment,the RMSlooks for changes inalldemand patterns. This check might lead to forecast changes that seem too large compared to the attribute change. For example, you change an attribute for a low-volume market segment. After the next optimization, you notice large changes in the demand forecast and in unrelated Forecast Groups.
The likely reason is thatthe RMSfound other changes in demand patterns. Those changes are not related to the attribute change, like an increase in business volume in a Forecast Group. Without the attribute change,the RMSwould have found that increase in its next regular check of demand patterns, seeSelf-Learningfor more details.

#### Monitoring Period for Forecast Group Changes

Let's say that you change the attribute of a market segment. The change causes the market segment to move to a different Forecast Group. In that case,the RMSmonitors the demand shift before it adjusts the impacted forecasts. This monitoring period of about two weeks ensures that the demand has shifted from one Forecast Group to another and is not due to a one-time event.
